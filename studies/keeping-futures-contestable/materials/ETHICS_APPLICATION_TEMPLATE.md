## Purpose and research question

The project examines how interface affordances during speculative making and question protocols during later evaluation influence whether assumptions about people, data, power, and responsibility remain noticeable, revisable, and open to further challenge.

The study does not assess participants' intelligence, morality, creativity, employability, or professional competence. The strongest planned claims are limited to the included participant groups, three visual micro-speculations, two implemented making workflows, two implemented evaluation protocols, and the recorded measures.

## Study components and planned sample

1. Cognitive pilot, excluded from the main dataset: 4–6 makers, 8–12 audience participants, and 2 expert reviewers.
2. Study A: 18 analysable makers in complete six-ID blocks, with at most one full replacement block and a maximum of 24.
3. Study B: planning floor of 128 analysable audience participants, approximately 144 recruits, with the final target and maximum frozen after pilot-informed crossed-data simulation. If infeasible, the study will be predeclared formative and will not support a population-level protocol-effect claim.
4. Expert audit: six analysable reviewers plus one reserve for the planned 36-item manifest; the target expands or the audit is narrowed before recruitment if eligible cell sizes change.

## What participants do

- Makers inspect two self-contained future artefacts, describe assumptions, respond to a stakeholder challenge, use a deterministic editing simulator for up to three candidates per task, and reflect on changed and unresolved assumptions.
- Audience participants evaluate three artefacts, one per brief, under one assigned question protocol and then answer the same neutral common probe.
- Experts review 12 blinded, technically valid artefacts for the stated speculative purpose and audience and select `KEEP`, `REVISE`, or `REJECT` with reasons.

Expected duration by component: `{{DURATION_BY_COMPONENT}}`

The interface uses fixed, pre-generated candidates for experimental control. It does not send participant text or artefacts to an external AI provider.

## Consent and withholding of directional hypotheses

Participants receive the approved information sheet and provide the institutionally approved form of consent before the app accepts an ID. The study purpose and procedures are disclosed, but the expected direction of condition differences and seeded assumptions are not revealed before task completion because that disclosure could directly alter the measured reasoning. The debrief explains the comparison and fixed-candidate simulator. This is not false feedback about performance.

## Foreseeable risks and mitigations

- Mild discomfort may arise when discussing unequal access, surveillance, care, climate adaptation, or institutional power. Participants may skip or stop according to the approved procedure.
- Free text may accidentally contain personal information. The app instructs participants not to enter names or contact details. Any affected return is stopped, quarantined, and treated under `{{PERSONAL_DATA_INCIDENT_PROCEDURE}}`.
- Facilitator demand effects are reduced through neutral prompts and a prohibited-coaching list.
- Raw data are not uploaded to model providers. Local software and deterministic checks do not replace investigator oversight.

## Stopping and amendments

Collection stops for withdrawal, distress, accidental personal-data entry, corrupted state, version/hash mismatch, or facilitator disclosure of a condition or hypothesis. Any change to approved wording, stimuli, app behaviour, sample plan, or data procedure is assessed under the institutional amendment route and receives a new version/freeze record before further collection.
